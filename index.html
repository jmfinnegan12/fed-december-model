Framework

How the FOMC decides

  • Twelve voters: the seven Board governors, the New York Fed president, and four of the other eleven Reserve Bank presidents in one-year rotation.11 In 2026 the rotating voters are Hammack (Cleveland), Kashkari (Minneapolis), Logan (Dallas) and Paulson (Philadelphia).15
  • Eight scheduled meetings a year. The decision is a change in the target range for the federal funds rate, announced at 2:00 pm ET on the meeting's second day, with each vote and dissent named.1,5
  • Participants make no policy remarks from the second Saturday before a meeting until the day after it.9

What it weighs

  • Two goals: maximum employment and stable prices. Inflation has a number, 2% a year in the PCE price index; maximum employment has none.12
  • When the goals conflict, a "balanced approach": how far each is from its goal, and how long each would take to return.12
  • Policy acts with a lag, so decisions look to the outlook and the balance of risks, including risks to the financial system. No formula sets the rate.12

Under Chair Warsh, since May 2026

  • His standard for action: "We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed." In September the Committee judged it not met and hiked.2 "Trends matter. Data points are noisy."2
  • The statement dropped its guidance on future moves in June.14 Warsh does not submit a projection for the dot plot.13 Signals now come from the minutes, the other participants' projections and voters' speeches.
